Project Overview:

With a historical chronology that spans more than 1,000 years, New Mexico is rich with complex layers of material culture. The historic sites in this region are all at different points in their preservation and idiosyncratic in their needs to be documented for continued research and public access.

Cultural SITEs set out to create digital records of historic sites and monuments, but after each visit and more experimentation with cutting-edge technology, the project grew into an archive of digital storytelling that speaks to the range of civilizations that have occupied this place. The SITEs team quickly learned that, because each site is unique in the stories it holds, there was no one-size-fits-all method for data collection. Photogrammetry and 360°-imaging methods were suitable for sites that were still standing and accessible to the public. Creating 3D models of these pueblos, buildings, monuments and artifacts allow for increased visitor access as well as a more versatile educational toolkit. LiDAR, ground penetrating radar and magnetometry were needed for gathering data on sites that were still beneath the surface of the Earth. Heat maps, point cloud models, digital terrain maps and more provided useful information that either corroborated previous research and/or assisted in further archaeological investigations.

Cultural SITEs also served as a bridge between innovative technology and the humanities in a broad sense. New Mexico, being home to such a deeply layered timeline of human culture, benefits massively from the latest tools in 3D-imaging. The SITEs project served the researchers and stewards of the region's history, and provided a proof-of-concept and justification for further use of these tools at even more historic sites around the state. The team learned both the limits of the technology and gained ideas for how much farther the exploration could go.
