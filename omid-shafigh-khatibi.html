Omid Shafigh Khatibi

Omid Shafigh Khatibi is an architectural designer and researcher pursuing a Master of Science in Architecture at the University of New Mexico, where he previously earned a Master of Architecture. His work explores the intersection of architecture, digital technologies, material innovation, and design, combining research with creative practice across multiple scales—from buildings and cultural heritage to furniture and product design. His current research investigates the reuse of waste soil from water treatment processes as a sustainable construction material, with a particular focus on earthen construction, material performance, and large-scale 3D printing applications.

Throughout his academic and professional career, Omid has contributed to a wide range of research, design, and fabrication projects. His work includes digital documentation, exhibition design, architectural visualization, 3D modeling, digital fabrication, and computational workflows. He contributed to the Palace Through Time exhibition, which explores the architectural evolution of the Palace of the Governors through digital reconstruction, visualization, and physical 3D-printed models. He has also worked with the University of New Mexico, the Indigenous Design + Planning Institute (iD+Pi), and interdisciplinary research teams on projects that integrate architecture, technology, and public engagement.

Beyond architecture, Omid has a strong interest in furniture design and fashion design, viewing both disciplines as opportunities to explore form, materiality, craftsmanship, and human experience at different scales. Whether designing buildings, furniture, wearable concepts, or digital experiences, he is driven by a passion for innovation, functionality, and thoughtful design.

His work has received national and international recognition, including the AIA Henry Adams Medal for excellence in graduation, the Excellence in Leadership Award, and recognition from the New Mexico History Museum. He has also received Honorable Mentions in two international architecture competitions, with both competition projects subsequently featured by ArchDaily. Omid is a member of both the Tau Sigma Delta Honor Society and the Golden Key International Honour Society, reflecting his commitment to academic excellence and professional achievement. He regularly presents his research at academic conferences, sharing work that bridges architecture, digital innovation, sustainable materials, and emerging fabrication technologies.
