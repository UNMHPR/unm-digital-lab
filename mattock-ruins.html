Mattocks Ruin

The Mimbres Cultural Heritage Site (Mimbres, New Mexico) is a nonprofit educational organization that uses a nineteenth-century homestead as a museum and headquarters for the Mattocks Ruin Mimbres Culture Archaeological Site. The Mattocks Ruin underwent two excavations, in the 1930s and 1970s, unearthing Mimbres pottery, though the pueblo structures have since been backfilled.

Cultural SITEs partnered with Altomaxx, a professional drone services company, to collect ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and magnetometry data on subterranean structures at the Mattocks Ruin. The project also documented the Gooch House homestead and the Doctor Wood Office using Matterport technology, creating immersive digital records that expand public access to one of New Mexico's most significant archaeological landscapes.

MIMBRES, NEW MEXICO—The Mattocks Ruin at the Mimbres Cultural Heritage Site was the fourth site selected for the Cultural SITEs project, with scanning completed in November 2024. The site preserves the remains of the Mimbres people, a branch of the larger Mogollon civilization that occupied southwestern New Mexico and southeastern Arizona between approximately AD 850 and 1000. The Mogollon remain among the most distinctive prehistoric cultures of the American Southwest, recognized for their finely crafted pottery, pueblo architecture, and mural traditions.

The Mattocks Ruin itself was first excavated during the late 1920s, uncovering an extensive collection of Mimbres pottery that was later distributed among several research institutions. A second excavation in the 1970s documented pueblo room blocks, pit houses, and associated structures before the site was once again carefully backfilled. Today the Mimbres Cultural Heritage Site remains open to the public, where interpretive models and walking trails help visitors visualize the buried archaeological remains.

Given that the pueblo structures at the Mattocks Ruin lie entirely underground, Cultural SITEs identified this as an appropriate site to test the capabilities of ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and magnetometry for subterranean data collection. Northrop Grumman's T4C team partnered with Altomaxx, a professional drone services company specializing in drone-mounted magnetometry and GPR sensors.

The director of the Mimbres Cultural Heritage Site noted that previous archaeological excavations had not extended north of the Mattocks Ruin, although researchers had long speculated that additional Mimbres pueblo structures might continue in that direction. Because the surrounding land is privately owned and includes an active residence, excavation is not a practical option, making remote sensing an attractive non-invasive alternative.

The team first conducted magnetometry surveys followed by GPR scans over the known Mattocks Ruin as a control area. The resulting data closely matched archaeological maps produced during the 1970s excavation, confirming the reliability of the sensing technologies. When surveys moved onto the adjacent private property, however, dense vegetation and environmental conditions interfered with the sensors, preventing meaningful penetration below the surface. Although these surveys did not reveal additional archaeological remains, the work successfully demonstrated both the capabilities and limitations of drone-based geophysical investigation and informed future Cultural SITEs field strategies.

In addition to the geophysical investigations, the Cultural SITEs team documented two historic buildings using Matterport technology: the Gooch House, which serves as the museum and visitor center, and the Wood House, a former doctor's residence now used by visiting researchers. These immersive digital models expand public access while preserving detailed architectural records for future research, education, and long-term documentation.
